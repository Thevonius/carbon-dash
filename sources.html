Every figure in the game comes from a published source below. The small numbers in the game (1, 2, 3…) match this list. Nothing here is unpublished or client data.

1. Towards positive energy district assessment: The case study of Bucharest

Sessa, Brunetti, Ciulla, Guarino, Longo, Cellura, Dragomir and Papina — Energy 325 (2025) 136208
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2025.136208

  • 447 buildings (443 residential, 4 schools) (Abstract)
  • Deep renovation cut heating demand by 24% (322.23 to 244.61 GWh/year) (Abstract, Table 7, Conclusions)
  • Electricity demand almost unchanged (351.19 to 350.27 GWh/year; Table 7 difference reported as 0%) (Abstract, Table 7)
  • Cooling demand −3% (121.88 to 118.36 GWh/year) (Table 7)
  • Methane for heating 43.4 to 32.9 million m³/year (−24%) (Table 7)
  • Potential rooftop PV 48.34 GWh/year, covering 13.8% of electricity demand (351 GWh) (Abstract, section 4.3.4, Conclusions)
  • Roof area 12% of total conditioned space (Abstract)

2. Climate neutral cities in the landscape of sustainability, climate justice, and energy

Sessa, Guarino, Cellura and Riva Sanseverino — Energy Reports (2025)
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.egyr.2025.10.025

  • 75% of people in the EU already live in cities, a share expected to reach 85% by 2050 (Introduction, citing European Commission, 2022)

3. Fundamental challenges in positive energy district implementation

Sessa, Di Pilla, Rincione, Brunetti, Guarino, Cellura, Longo and Riva Sanseverino — J. Sustainability for Energy 3 (4) 234–243 (2024)
https://doi.org/10.56578/jse030403

  • PEDs: energy-efficient, energy-flexible urban areas with net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions and an annual surplus of local or regional renewable energy (Section 1, quoted definition)
  • Cities occupy 2% of the world’s land and about 50% of its people, expected to reach 68% by 2050 (Introduction, citing United Nations, 2018)

4. Redesign of a rural building in a heritage site in Italy: towards the net zero energy target

Cellura, Ciulla, Guarino and Longo — Buildings 7 (3) 68 (2017)
https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings7030068

  • Lighting changes cut energy use by up to 30% (Table 1: 30.02% overall, 59.10% for lighting) (Abstract and Table 1)
  • Better set-points or natural ventilation cut energy use by up to 10% (Table 1: 5.50% and 9.92%) (Abstract and Table 1)

5. Fostering the energy transition in a neighbourhood perspective: towards Positive Energy Districts’ application for a university case study

Di Pilla, Cellura, Franzitta, Guarino, Longo, Curto, Ilardi and Panno — Energy Reports (June 2025) 5628–5652
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.egyr.2025.05.012

  • Renovation cut annual energy demand by 40% (Abstract)
  • On-site generation could supply up to 80% of annual energy demand (Abstract)
  • Not sufficient, on its own, to reach PED standards (Abstract)
  • Life-cycle energy −63%; global warming potential −34% (Abstract)

6. 2050 long-term strategy (EU climate-neutrality goal)

European Commission — official EU climate strategy
https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/climate-strategies-targets/2050-long-term-strategy_en

  • The EU aims to be climate-neutral by 2050 (EU long-term strategy / European Climate Law)

7. Leveraging Positive Energy Districts surplus for the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals

Cellura, Fichera, Guarino and Volpe — Energies 18 (3) 506 (2025)
https://doi.org/10.3390/en18030506

  • Surplus electricity could support up to 30 low-income households (Abstract)
  • Estimated annual saving of EUR 1,145 per household (Abstract)

8. Our Common Future (Brundtland Report)

World Commission on Environment and Development — United Nations, 1987
https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/139811

  • Definition of sustainable development quoted in the intro story (1987 report, used in the story as a quotation)

9. A simplified tool for early-stage planning of positive energy districts (TREEPED)

Brunetti, Guarino, Ciulla, Cellura, Díaz de Garayo and Longo — Energy Reports (2025)
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.egyr.2025.04.059

  • Renovated scenario: heating demand −17%, cooling −84%, electricity +43% (Abstract, San Pedro, Pamplona case)
  • With a nearby PV system the district exceeded the PED balance by 0.67 GWh (Abstract)
  • Life-cycle assessment: Global Warming Potential −60%; Cumulative Energy Demand −69% (Abstract)
